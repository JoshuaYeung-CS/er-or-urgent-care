How to read these numbers

What the hospital number is

CMS measure OP-18b: the median minutes from arriving at the emergency department to leaving it, for patients who were sent home (discharged). It excludes patients admitted to the hospital, transferred, or seen for mental-health care. Reporting period: . Hospitals report it from a sample of cases; the case count is shown on each card.

What it is not
  • Not the time until you see a clinician. It is the whole visit: triage, waiting, tests, treatment, discharge.
  • Not live. It summarizes a 12-month period that ended . Tonight can be very different (time of day, flu season, a bad crash nearby).
  • Not your time. Sicker patients are seen first. A sprained ankle may wait longer than the median; chest pain should not wait at all.
Where the ranges come from (and why they are assumptions)

CMS publishes one number per hospital and no spread. To show what a typical spread might look like, I model one visit's duration as a lognormal with its median fixed at the hospital's OP-18b value and a dispersion σ that I chose (default 0.6, with 0.4 to 0.8 shown as the lighter band). The lognormal is a common right-skewed shape for durations: most visits cluster near the median and a long tail runs late. No paper is cited for σ; treat every range and every "chance over 4 hours" as conditional on it. The median itself is solid data; the range is illustration.

The "std. error of median" next to the case count is the statistical precision of the median itself (large-sample standard error of a median, m·σ·√(π / 2n)), which is small next to the visit-to-visit spread.

Data limits
  • Locations are ZIP centroids from the U.S. Census 2025 ZCTA gazetteer, so distances are approximate. hospitals have a ZIP that is not a Census ZCTA (often a PO-box ZIP) and are left out of distance results.
  • Hospitals that CMS lists without emergency services are hidden from the list.
  • Urgent-care listings exist only for the five demo areas, from a single OpenStreetMap snapshot. Tagging varies, so some clinics are missing.
  • No urgent-care wait data exists publicly. I do not estimate one.
